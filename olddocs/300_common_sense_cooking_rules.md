# 300 Common Sense Cooking Rules

## 1. Preparation & Organization (Mise en Place)
1. **Read the whole recipe**: Read it completely before you touch a single ingredient.
2. **Prep before heat**: Chop, measure, and assemble everything before turning on the stove.
3. **Baking is chemistry**: Weigh baking ingredients with a scale; do not scoop and guess.
4. **Room temperature rule**: Bring meat, eggs, and butter to room temp before cooking or baking.
5. **Clear your station**: Wash dirty dishes and clear your counters before you start cooking.
6. **The trash bowl**: Keep a large bowl on your counter for food scraps and peels.
7. **Plug in safely**: Only plug in blenders and food processors once they are fully assembled.
8. **Plan your timing**: Start dishes with the longest cook times first.
9. **Keep it cold**: Leave fish and meat in the fridge until the exact moment you need them.
10. **Recipes are guides**: Treat cooking recipes like roadmaps, but baking recipes like laws.
11. **Organize by sequence**: Line up your prepped ingredients in the order they go into the pan.
12. **Sharpen your pencils**: Check that you have all necessary tools clean and ready before starting.
13. **Labels matter**: Label and date containers before putting them in the freezer.
14. **Check your inventory**: Ensure you actually have an ingredient before you start cooking with it.
15. **Pre-read instructions**: Check if a recipe requires overnight marinating or chilling.
16. **Know your oven**: Figure out where your oven's hot spots are by watching how toast browns.
17. **Set timers**: Never rely on your memory to track bake or roast times.
18. **Keep tools close**: Hang or store your most-used spoons, tongs, and spatulas within arm's reach.
19. **Unwrap completely**: Remove all plastic, stickers, and tags from produce before prepping.
20. **Stay focused**: Do not start a complex new recipe when you are tired or deeply distracted.
21. **Group similar tasks**: Peel all your garlic, onions, and shallots in one go to minimize cleanup.
22. **Check the yield**: Verify how many servings a recipe makes before buying ingredients.
23. **Plan for leftovers**: Prepare larger batches of grains or proteins to save time later in the week.
24. **Keep appliances clean**: Empty the crumb tray of your toaster and wipe down the microwave regularly.
25. **Store instructions near**: Keep your tablet or recipe book at eye level, away from splashes.
26. **Unpack groceries smart**: Put away perishable items first before sorting pantry staples.
27. **Set up multi-cooking**: If using the oven and stove, set separate distinct timers for each.
28. **Pre-heat surfaces**: Heat your pizza stone or baking steel for at least 45 minutes before using.
29. **Assess cooking vessels**: Make sure your pot is big enough for the soup to bubble without spilling over.
30. **Mind the lid**: Keep matching lids next to your pots before you start cooking.

## 2. Knife Safety & Technique
31. **Sharp knives only**: Dull knives slip easily and cause the worst cuts.
32. **The claw grip**: Curl the fingers of your holding hand inward like a claw to protect them.
33. **Secure your board**: Place a damp paper towel or silicone mat under your cutting board so it cannot slide.
34. **Match size to task**: Use a large chef’s knife for big vegetables, not a tiny paring knife.
35. **Never hide knives**: Do not drop knives into soapy, cloudy sink water where you cannot see them.
36. **Walk with care**: Carry knives close to your leg with the tip pointing straight down.
37. **Let it fall**: Never try to catch a falling knife; step back and let it hit the floor.
38. **No substitution**: Never use a knife blade as a can opener, screwdriver, or pry bar.
39. **Protect the edge**: Use the spine of your knife to scrape food off a board, never the sharp edge.
40. **Hand wash blades**: Always wash good knives by hand; never put them in the dishwasher.
41. **Cut away**: Always cut away from your body when peeling or trimming.
42. **Flat surfaces first**: Slice a small piece off round vegetables (like onions) to create a flat, stable base.
43. **Keep fingers clear**: Keep your thumb tucked behind your fingers when holding food to slice.
44. **Blade exposure**: Store knives in a block, on a magnetic strip, or in protective sheaths.
45. **Keep your eyes down**: Never look away from your hands while actively chopping.
46. **No forcing**: If you have to hack or saw violently, your knife is too dull or too small.
47. **Cut on wood or plastic**: Never cut food directly on stone, glass, or stainless steel counters.
48. **Pass with care**: Hand a knife to someone by setting it down on the counter, or by holding the spine.
49. **Clean immediately**: Wipe your knife blade immediately after slicing acidic foods like lemons.
50. **Dominant hand control**: Grip the knife firmly where the blade meets the handle for maximum control.
51. **Slice, don't push**: Use a rocking motion or a smooth back-and-forth slice instead of crushing downward.
52. **Keep the tip down**: When dicing quickly, keep the tip of the knife in constant contact with the board.
53. **Dry your hands**: Never chop food with wet, oily, or soapy hands.
54. **Don't gesture with knives**: Put the knife down before using your hands to talk or point.
55. **Cut uniform thickness**: Focus on thickness consistency rather than speed when slicing.
56. **Chop heavy root vegetables carefully**: Take extra care with dense vegetables like butternut squash or sweet potatoes.
57. **Keep children away**: Never leave a knife resting near the edge of a counter within a child's reach.
58. **Do not use a serrated knife for everything**: Save serrated blades for bread, tomatoes, and citrus fruits.
59. **Clean the board frequently**: Wipe away built-up vegetable skins and juices to keep your cutting surface safe.
60. **Maintain the angle**: When honing your knife, hold the blade at a consistent 15 to 20-degree angle.

## 3. Heat & Pan Control
61. **Preheat the pan**: Let your pan get hot before you add any oil or fat.
62. **Shimmering oil**: Wait until the oil shimmers or thins out before dropping food into it.
63. **Don't crowd the pan**: Leaving space allows food to brown; crowding makes it steam and get soggy.
64. **Turn handles in**: Turn pot handles toward the back or side of the stove so they cannot be bumped.
65. **Dry your food**: Pat meat and fish thoroughly dry with paper towels so they sear instead of boiling.
66. **Respect the smoke point**: Do not use delicate extra virgin olive oil for high-heat searing.
67. **Listen for the sizzle**: If food does not sizzle when it hits the pan, the pan is too cold.
68. **Leave it alone**: Do not constantly flip or stir meat; let it sit undisturbed to develop a crust.
69. **Carryover cooking**: Food keeps cooking after it leaves the heat; pull it off just before it is perfectly done.
70. **Preheat your oven**: Wait for the oven to reach full temperature before putting food inside.
71. **Match burner to pot**: Use a burner that matches the diameter of your pot base to save energy and prevent burning.
72. **Vent early**: Turn on your exhaust fan before you start searing or frying, not after the kitchen fills with smoke.
73. **Dry towels only**: Never use a wet towel or wet oven mitt to grab a hot pan; the water turns to burning steam.
74. **Don't walk away**: Never leave a stove burner or broiler unattended.
75. **Lower the heat**: If something is burning, turn down the dial immediately instead of just stirring faster.
76. **Thick bottoms win**: Use heavy-bottomed pans because they distribute heat evenly and prevent hot spots.
77. **Deglaze the pan**: Use wine, broth, or water to scrape up the delicious browned bits at the bottom of a pan.
78. **Boil with a lid**: Pots boil significantly faster with the lid kept on.
79. **Simmer vs. Boil**: A simmer has gentle, lazy bubbles; a boil is aggressive. Match the recipe's request.
80. **Cool pans slowly**: Never plunge a scorching hot pan into cold sink water; it will warp or crack.
81. **Know your pan materials**: Use stainless steel for searing, non-stick for eggs, and cast iron for high heat retention.
82. **Adjust for induction**: Induction cooktops heat up instantly; do not leave an empty pan on a high induction burner.
83. **Check broiler clearance**: Ensure your food won't physically touch the heating elements when using the broiler.
84. **Use high sides for frying**: Fry foods in deep pots to prevent oil from splattering over the sides.
85. **Don't overheat non-stick**: Cooking on maximum heat can damage non-stick coatings and release toxic fumes.
86. **Preheat baking sheets**: Put your sheet pan in the oven while it heats up to get instant browning on roasted veggies.
87. **Use a splatter screen**: Protect your stovetop and skin with a fine mesh screen when frying.
88. **Bake on the middle rack**: Default to the center of the oven for even heat distribution from top and bottom.
89. **Control the flame**: Gas flames should never lick up past the vertical sides of your pot or pan.
90. **Let elements cool down**: Remember that electric coil burners stay dangerously hot long after you turn them off.

## 4. Seasoning & Flavor Building
91. **Season in layers**: Add small amounts of salt throughout the cooking process, not just at the very end.
92. **Taste as you go**: Always taste your food multiple times before serving it.
93. **Acid saves the day**: If a dish tastes flat but has enough salt, add a splash of lemon juice or vinegar.
94. **Fixing salty food**: Dilute over-salted soups with water, broth, or a raw starch like a potato.
95. **Fresh herbs at the end**: Add soft, delicate herbs (like parsley or cilantro) in the final minute of cooking.
96. **Hard herbs cook long**: Add woody herbs (like rosemary and thyme) early so they have time to soften and release oils.
97. **Bloom your spices**: Cook dry spices in oil or butter for 30 seconds to double their flavor.
98. **Salt pasta water heavily**: Your pasta water should taste aggressively salty, like the ocean.
99. **Freshly ground pepper**: Pre-ground black pepper tastes like dust; use a pepper mill for real flavor.
100. **A pinch of sugar**: A tiny pinch of sugar cuts the harsh acidity in tomato-based sauces.
101. **Umami boosters**: Use soy sauce, fish sauce, tomato paste, or parmesan rind to add deep, savory richness.
102. **Zest before juicing**: Always grate the skin off citrus fruits before squeezing out the juice.
103. **Fat equals flavor**: Do not skim every drop of fat; fat carries flavor and creates a smooth mouthfeel.
104. **Toast your nuts**: Toast nuts and seeds in a dry pan until fragrant before adding them to a dish.
105. **Bacon fat is gold**: Save clean bacon grease in a jar in the fridge to fry vegetables later.
106. **Salt extracts moisture**: Salting mushrooms or eggplant early draws out water; salt them late if you want a hard sear.
107. **Cold mutes flavor**: Food tastes less vibrant when freezing cold; season your cold dishes slightly more aggressively.
108. **Incorporate garlic late**: Garlic burns quickly and turns bitter; add it after your onions have softened.
109. **Do not rush onions**: True caramelized onions take at least 30 to 45 minutes; you cannot rush them with high heat.
110. **Taste the raw components**: Taste your ingredients individually to understand how intense they are before mixing them.
111. **Balance bitter with fat**: Use olive oil or butter to soften the sharp bite of bitter greens like kale or escarole.
112. **Grind whole spices**: Buy whole cumin, coriander, and peppercorns and crush them yourself for superior aroma.
113. **Don't use old spices**: Throw away spices that have sat in your cabinet for over a year; they lose all punch.
114. **Salt meat early**: Dry-brining steaks or chicken with salt hours before cooking lets the flavor penetrate to the bone.
115. **Use MSG safely**: A tiny pinch of monosodium glutamate (MSG) provides pure umami without harming flavor balance.
116. **Deglaze with alcohol carefully**: Turn off gas flames before pouring wine or liquor into a hot pan to prevent flare-ups.
117. **Understand vinegar profiles**: Match delicate rice vinegar with Asian dishes and robust red wine vinegar with hearty stews.
118. **Steep dry mushrooms**: Soak dried porcini or shiitake mushrooms in hot water and use that liquid as a savory flavor base.
119. **Sweeten with honey or maple**: Use natural liquid sweeteners for dressings to help them emulsify smoothly.
120. **Finish with flaky salt**: Use expensive sea salt flakes right at the table for a pleasant crunch and clean bursts of salt.

## 5. Food Safety & Sanitation
121. **Wash your hands**: Wash them thoroughly before cooking and immediately after touching raw meat.
122. **Separate boards**: Dedicate one cutting board strictly for raw proteins and another for ready-to-eat foods.
123. **Never wash chicken**: Rinsing raw poultry splashes invisible bacteria all over your sink and countertops.
124. **Know your temps**: Cook poultry to 165°F (74°C) and ground meats to 160°F (71°C) to ensure safety.
125. **Cycle your towels**: Swap out kitchen towels and dish sponges every couple of days.
126. **Cool before closing**: Let hot leftovers vent and cool slightly before sealing them in containers and refrigerating.
127. **The 2-hour rule**: Never leave cooked food sitting out at room temperature for more than two hours.
128. **Thaw in the fridge**: Defrost frozen meats slowly in the refrigerator, never on the warm kitchen counter.
129. **Tie back hair**: Secure long hair and roll up loose sleeves before you start working near food or flames.
130. **Cover wounds**: Never cook for others with an open cut on your hand; use a clean bandage and a glove.
131. **Sanitize counters**: Wipe down your workspaces with an antibacterial cleaner before and after cooking.
132. **Check expiration dates**: Read use-by dates on dairy and seafood before building a meal around them.
133. **When in doubt, throw it out**: If food smells off, looks slimy, or has an unnatural color, discard it.
134. **Wash your produce**: Rinse all fruits and vegetables under cold water to remove dirt, pesticides, and bugs.
135. **Store meat on the bottom**: Keep raw meat on the lowest shelf of the fridge so it cannot drip onto other food.
136. **Use clean spoons**: Never double-dip a spoon back into a communal pot after tasting from it.
137. **Can safety**: Never use food from cans that are deeply dented, bulging, or leaking.
138. **Clean can tops**: Wipe the dust off the top of a tin can before opening it.
139. **Watch the marinade**: Never reuse a liquid marinade that touched raw meat as a table sauce unless you boil it first.
140. **Egg safety**: Do not use cracked or leaking eggs straight out of the carton.
141. **Keep hot foods hot**: Maintain buffet or party foods above 140°F (60°C) if serving over several hours.
142. **Keep cold foods cold**: Set salad bowls or seafood platters over ice beds at outdoor summer gatherings.
143. **Sanitize can openers**: Clean the cutting wheel of your can opener regularly to prevent mold accumulation.
144. **Avoid cross-contamination via hands**: Don't open the fridge door or touch spice jars right after handling raw meat.
145. **Cool large batches fast**: Divide giant pots of chili or stew into smaller, shallow pans so they chill rapidly.
146. **Check shellfish**: Fresh clams and mussels should be tightly closed before cooking; discard any that stay open.
147. **Discard broken shells**: Toss out any cooked clams or mussels that fail to open up after steaming.
148. **Reheat thoroughly**: Bring leftover soups and gravies to a rolling boil before consuming them.
149. **Mind the danger zone**: Bacteria multiply fastest between 40°F (4°C) and 140°F (60°C). Minimize time spent here.
150. **Do not raw-taste ground meat**: Never taste raw ground beef, pork, or turkey mixes to check for salt seasoning.

## 6. Cleaning & Maintenance
151. **Clean as you go**: Wash mixing bowls and measuring cups while your food is simmering or baking.
152. **Soak immediately**: Pour warm water into sticky or cheesy pots immediately after emptying them.
153. **Cast iron care**: Wash cast iron with minimal soap, dry it completely on the stove, and rub it with a tiny drop of oil.
154. **Wipe spills fast**: Clean stovetop splatters before they have a chance to bake into hard, crusty rings.
155. **Keep wood out of the machine**: Never put wooden cutting boards or wooden spoons in the dishwasher.
156. **Empty the trash**: Take out smelly kitchen scraps immediately so your kitchen stays fresh.
157. **Never pour grease down the drain**: Pour cooking oil into an old jar or can, let it harden, and throw it in the trash.
158. **Baking soda for burns**: Lift stubborn burned-on food by boiling water and baking soda inside the pot.
159. **Clean the fridge**: Wipe down your refrigerator shelves once a month to prevent mold and odors.
160. **Dry copper immediately**: Dry copper pots right after washing to prevent tarnishing and spotting.
161. **Sharpen vs. Hone**: Hone your knife with a steel weekly; sharpen it with a stone once or twice a year.
162. **Blender cleaning hack**: Fill a dirty blender halfway with warm water and a drop of soap, then blend it clean.
163. **Clean under appliances**: Wipe down the counter spaces underneath your toaster, microwave, and coffee maker.
164. **Scrub the sink**: Your sink holds a lot of bacteria; scrub it down with soap and disinfectant regularly.
165. **Oil your boards**: Rub food-grade mineral oil onto wooden cutting boards monthly to keep them from cracking.
166. **Microwave steam trick**: Microwave a bowl of water with lemon slices for 3 minutes to easily wipe away stuck-on food.
167. **Shake out the toaster**: Regularly empty the crumb tray at the bottom of your toaster to avoid fire hazards.
168. **Scrape plates clean**: Scrape all solid food scraps into the trash before placing plates in the sink or dishwasher.
169. **Organize your lids**: Keep plastic container lids matched up or organized so you don't waste time searching.
170. **Wash the can opener**: Can openers accumulate old food residue; wash them frequently.
171. **De-scale your kettle**: Run vinegar and water through your electric kettle to remove hard white calcium scaling.
172. **Clean kitchen exhaust grids**: Soak grease filters from your range hood in soapy water to keep air flowing.
173. **Polish stainless steel**: Use a tiny dab of mineral oil on a microfiber cloth to wipe away fingerprints from appliances.
174. **Keep sponges dry**: Wring out sponges completely and store them in a well-ventilated rack so they dry out fast.
175. **Clean your dishwasher filter**: Twist out the filter at the bottom of your dishwasher weekly and rinse away food debris.
176. **Use nylon pads on non-stick**: Never use steel wool or metal scrubbers on delicate non-stick pan coatings.
177. **Clear garbage disposal buildup**: Grind ice cubes and lemon peels inside the disposal unit to clean the blades and remove odors.
178. **Clean spice grinders**: Grind a tablespoon of dry white rice to clean oily residues out of your coffee/spice mill.
179. **Wash oven mitts**: Fabric oven mitts accumulate grease over time; toss them into the washing machine periodically.
180. **Sanitize trash cans**: Spray the interior of your kitchen garbage bins with disinfectant whenever you change bags.

## 7. Baking Foundations
181. **No peeking**: Do not constantly open the oven door when baking cakes or soufflés, or the cold air will deflate them.
182. **The crack-separate rule**: Crack eggs into a small, separate bowl before adding them to your main batter.
183. **Sift for smoothness**: Sift flour and powdered sugar when making delicate cakes to avoid stubborn lumps.
184. **Do not overmix**: Mix muffin and pancake batter just until the flour disappears; overmixing makes them tough and rubbery.
185. **The toothpick test**: Insert a wooden toothpick into the center of a cake; if it comes out clean, the cake is done.
186. **Let it cool**: Never slice hot bread or frost a warm cake; let them cool completely first.
187. **Parchment paper is a savior**: Line baking sheets with parchment paper to prevent sticking and make cleaning effortless.
188. **Grease and flour**: Grease baking pans thoroughly, then dust them with flour or cocoa powder to ensure easy release.
189. **Yeast needs warmth**: Use lukewarm liquid for yeast; boiling water kills it, and ice-cold water won't activate it.
190. **Sweet needs salt**: Always add a tiny pinch of salt to sweet doughs and batters to enhance the sugars.
191. **Flour your tools**: Dust your hands, rolling pin, and counter with flour when working with sticky dough.
192. **Use fresh leaveners**: Baking powder and baking soda lose their potency after 6 months; replace them regularly.
193. **Spoon and level**: If you must use measuring cups for flour, spoon it in gently and level it with a knife. Never scoop from the bag.
194. **Don't skip the chill**: Chill cookie dough if the recipe asks for it; it prevents the cookies from spreading into flat puddles.
195. **Center rack default**: Always bake on the middle rack of the oven unless the recipe specifies otherwise.
196. **Softened vs. Melted**: If a recipe calls for softened butter, do not microwave it into a liquid puddle.
197. **Scrape the bowl**: Stop your mixer occasionally to scrape the bottom of the bowl with a rubber spatula.
198. **Cool on wire racks**: Move baked goods to a wire rack so air can circulate underneath, preventing soggy bottoms.
199. **Whisk dry ingredients**: Whisk your flour, salt, and baking powder together before adding wet ingredients to ensure even distribution.
200. **Measure liquids at eye level**: Place liquid measuring cups on a flat counter and bend down to check the line.
201. **Check expiration dates on yeast**: Old yeast won't rise, leaving your bread dense, heavy, and brick-like.
202. **Bake by weight consistency**: Weigh out individual dough portions for dinner rolls so they bake at an identical rate.
203. **Avoid drafting dough**: Keep rising bread dough in a warm, draft-free spot covered with a clean damp cloth.
204. **Don't over-inflate dough**: Letting bread rise for too long causes it to collapse completely once inside the hot oven.
205. **Understand pan colors**: Dark metal pans absorb heat quickly and brown crusts faster than shiny aluminum or glass pans.
206. **Calibrate your oven thermometer**: Built-in oven dials are notoriously inaccurate; hang a cheap standalone thermometer inside.
207. **Tuck in your pie crust**: Don't stretch pie dough to fit the pan; gently tuck it down into the corners so it doesn't shrink during baking.
208. **Blind bake wet pies**: Pre-bake custard and fruit pie shells slightly before adding wet fillings to avoid a soggy bottom crust.
209. **Don't skip the rest for pie dough**: Wrap pastry dough in plastic and chill it for 30 minutes to relax the gluten before rolling.
210. **Use unsalted butter by default**: Unsalted butter allows you to control the exact salt levels of your baked goods.

## 8. Meat, Fish & Proteins
211. **Rest your meat**: Let steaks and roasts rest for 5 to 10 minutes after cooking so the delicious juices don't run out.
212. **Against the grain**: Always slice meat perpendicular to the visible muscle fibers to make it tender to chew.
213. **Skin-side first**: Cook fish skin-side down first to get a crispy exterior and prevent the delicate flesh from breaking.
214. **Low and slow eggs**: Cook scrambled eggs over low heat while stirring constantly for a rich, creamy texture.
215. **The shrimp "C" rule**: Remove shrimp from heat when they curl into a "C" shape; an "O" shape means they are overcooked.
216. **Give marinade time**: Give marinades a few hours to penetrate meat; a 5-minute soak does nothing.
217. **Delay the acid**: Add acidic components like tomatoes or vinegar late to stews, as acid can keep meat tough if added too early.
218. **Broth over water**: Braise and simmer meats in broth or stock rather than plain water for much better flavor.
219. **Cold bacon start**: Lay bacon strips into a cold skillet, then turn on the heat to render out the fat cleanly.
220. **Ground meat browning**: Press ground meat flat into a hot pan like a giant patty, let a deep crust form, then break it up.
221. **Baste with butter**: Spoon hot, foaming butter and herbs over steaks or chops during the last minute of cooking.
222. **Buy bone-in**: Cooking meat on the bone yields more flavor and helps prevent the meat from drying out.
223. **Don't press burgers**: Never press down on burgers with a spatula while they cook; you are squeezing out the juices.
224. **Don't boil meat**: Simmer stews gently; boiling meat rapidly makes the muscle fibers curl up tight and turn stringy.
225. **Peel shrimp cold**: Shrimp are much easier to peel and de-vein when they are ice-cold.
226. **Check for fish bones**: Run your fingers firmly across raw fish fillets to find and remove tiny pin-bones before cooking.
227. **Render the fat cap**: Hold thick steaks vertically with tongs to sear and melt the thick strip of fat on the edge.
228. **Don't overcook pork**: Modern pork does not need to be cooked until it resembles cardboard; a slight pink blush is safe and juicy.
229. **Pat chicken skin dry**: For ultra-crispy roasted chicken skin, leave it uncovered in the fridge for a few hours to dry out.
230. **Trust the thermometer**: Don't guess if meat is done by cutting into it; use a digital meat thermometer.
231. **Slice chicken breasts horizontally**: Cut thick chicken breasts into thin cutlets so they cook evenly without drying out.
232. **Use cornstarch on stir-fry meat**: Toss thin meat slices in a bit of cornstarch (velveting) to keep them incredibly tender.
233. **Don't poke cooking meat with forks**: Use tongs to flip meat; stabbing it with a fork creates holes for juices to escape.
234. **Score thick skins**: Cut shallow diamond patterns into duck breasts or pork roasts to help fat render and prevent curling.
235. **Keep fish cold until cooking**: Fish spoils quickly; keep it on ice or at the back of the fridge until the pan is hot.
236. **Blanch bones for clear stock**: Boil beef or chicken bones for 10 minutes and rinse them before simmering your main stock.
237. **Skim stock scum**: Ladle out the gray foam that rises to the top of simmering stocks to keep the liquid clear and clean-tasting.
238. **Don't over-handle burger meat**: Mix ground beef gently; overworking the meat creates dense, tough, rubbery burger patties.
239. **Searing does not lock in juices**: Searing creates flavor via browning; resting is what actually locks in the juices.
240. **Match protein to cooking method**: Braise tough cuts (like chuck roast) long and slow; cook tender cuts (like ribeye) hot and fast.

## 9. Vegetables & Side Dishes
241. **Uniform cuts**: Slice your vegetables into identical sizes so they all finish cooking at the exact same moment.
242. **Shock green veggies**: Plunge boiled green vegetables (like broccoli or green beans) into ice water to lock in their bright color.
243. **Cold water potato start**: Always place potatoes in cold, salted water and bring them to a boil together so they cook evenly.
244. **Don't wash mushrooms**: Wipe mushrooms clean with a dry paper towel; rinsing them causes them to absorb water like a sponge.
245. **Do not stir rice**: Leave cooking rice alone under a tight lid; stirring releases starch and makes it sticky and gummy.
246. **Save pasta water**: Scoop out a cup of starchy pasta cooking water before draining; it is the secret to smooth, glossy sauces.
247. **Onions before garlic**: Sauté onions first until clear, then add garlic for the last 60 seconds so it doesn't burn.
248. **Roast for sweetness**: Roast root vegetables in the oven instead of boiling them to caramelize their natural sugars.
249. **Salt beans late**: Add salt to dry beans and lentils after they have softened up, or their skins may stay tough.
250. **Spin your greens**: Use a salad spinner to dry your greens thoroughly, or your dressing will slide right off the wet leaves.
251. **De-seed watery veggies**: Scoop the watery seeds out of tomatoes and cucumbers if you are making a crisp salad.
252. **High heat for mushrooms**: Cook mushrooms in a hot pan with plenty of space so they brown beautifully instead of boiling in their own juices.
253. **Steam off potatoes**: Let boiled potatoes steam dry in a colander for a few minutes before mashing them for a fluffier texture.
254. **Don't peel everything**: The skins of clean carrots, cucumbers, and young potatoes hold tons of flavor and nutrients.
255. **Pierce potatoes**: Always poke holes in whole potatoes with a fork before baking them to let steam escape.
256. **Salt eggplant**: Sprinkle salt on sliced eggplant and let it sit to drain away bitter juices before cooking.
257. **Slice cabbage thin**: Shred cabbage as finely as possible for slaws so it can absorb the dressing quickly.
258. **Sauté greens in batches**: Pile leafy greens like spinach into the pan in stages; they wilt down to a fraction of their size.
259. **Cut corn safely**: Lay a cob of corn flat or steady it inside a wide bowl to slice off the kernels without them flying everywhere.
260. **Asparagus snap**: Snap the woody ends off asparagus by bending them near the base; they will naturally break where the tender part starts.
261. **Don't boil broccoli to mush**: Cook broccoli until it is fork-tender but still retains a bright green color and slight bite.
262. **Sweat your aromatics**: Cook celery, carrots, and onions slowly over low heat with butter to create a rich flavor foundation (mirepoix).
263. **Use acid on avocados**: Toss diced avocado with lime juice immediately to prevent it from oxidizing and turning brown.
264. **Rinse starchy rice**: Wash raw rice in a bowl of cold water several times until the water runs clear to prevent clumping.
265. **Dress salad right before serving**: Putting dressing on salad hours early turns the leaves into a wilted, soggy mess.
266. **Grate cheese yourself**: Pre-shredded cheese is coated in starch to prevent sticking, which stops it from melting smoothly.
267. **Soak raw onions**: Steep sliced raw onions in ice water for 10 minutes to wash away their harsh, burning bite before putting them in salads.
268. **Don't crowd roasting veggies**: Spread vegetables out completely on sheet pans so they roast crisp instead of steaming soft.
269. **Toss pasta inside the sauce pan**: Never dump plain pasta on a plate and pour sauce on top; combine them in the pan with pasta water.
270. **Cook grains by ratio**: Always use exact water-to-grain liquid ratios for quinoa, couscous, and farro to avoid mushiness.

## 10. Troubleshooting & Kitchen Logistics
271. **Never water a grease fire**: Putting water on an oil fire creates an explosion. Smother it with a lid or baking soda.
272. **Treat cuts fast**: Wash a cut instantly, apply a tight bandage, put on a kitchen glove, and then return to work.
273. **Cool burns immediately**: Run kitchen burns under cool, flowing tap water for several minutes. Never apply butter to a burn.
274. **Freeze flat**: Freeze soups, stews, and sauces flat in heavy-duty plastic freezer bags to save space and speed up thawing.
275. **Trust your nose**: If something smells foul, sour, or weirdly chemical, trust your basic survival instincts and throw it away.
276. **Fix a thin sauce**: Thicken runny sauces by letting them simmer down, or stir in a slurry of cornstarch and cold water.
277. **Cool down spice**: Tame a dish that is way too spicy by serving it with dairy, fat, sugar, or an extra splash of acid.
278. **Revive stale bread**: Splash a stale loaf of bread with water and bake it at 350°F (175°C) for 5 minutes to make it crunchy again.
279. **Save cheese scraps**: Grate leftover hard cheese ends into a bag and store them in the freezer for future gratins or pastas.
280. **Don't panic**: Almost every single kitchen mistake can be countered or balanced with a bit of acid, fat, sugar, or water.
281. **Fix broken sauces**: Repair a split mayonnaise or hollandaise by slowly whisking it into a fresh egg yolk or a teaspoon of warm water.
282. **Burnt bottom rescue**: If the bottom of your soup pot burns, do not scrape the bottom. Pour the unburnt top layers into a clean pot.
283. **Keep baking soda nearby**: Keep an open box of baking soda near the stove to put out unexpected minor fires.
284. **Sharpen your scissors**: Kitchen shears are incredibly useful; treat them well and keep them sharp.
285. **Don't double recipes blindly**: When doubling a recipe, do not automatically double the salt or spices; add them gradually to taste.
286. **Keep a kitchen notebook**: Write down your adjustments, cooking times, and flavor successes so you can replicate them later.
287. **Label your spices**: Write the purchase date on spice jars so you know when they have gone stale.
288. **Clear the oven first**: Check inside the oven to make sure no pans or storage containers are hidden inside before turning it on.
289. **Watch the airflow**: Keep your kitchen window or back door cracked if you are doing heavy indoor grilling or frying.
290. **Enjoy the process**: Cooking is a skill learned through repetition; accept mistakes as valuable lessons and keep going.
291. **Fix over-sweetened food**: Add a small squeeze of lemon juice, a dash of vinegar, or a pinch of salt to tone down excess sweetness.
292. **Soften hard brown sugar**: Place a damp paper towel or an apple wedge into the airtight container with the sugar overnight.
293. **Keep grease fires covered**: Keep a flat baking sheet or large metal lid right next to your frying station for instant smothering power.
294. **Thicken soup with potatoes**: Blend a boiled potato back into a watery vegetable soup to add body without altering the flavor profile.
295. **Defrost sauces safely**: Submerge sealed frozen sauce bags in hot tap water for rapid, safe thawing.
296. **De-clump salt shakers**: Put a few grains of uncooked dry white rice into your salt shaker to absorb ambient humidity.
297. **Keep emergency provisions**: Always keep dry pasta, canned tomatoes, and eggs on hand for a quick, effortless meal anytime.
298. **Fix bitter dishes**: Smooth out an overly bitter sauce or soup by melting in a small pat of butter or heavy cream.
299. **Wipe up oil instantly**: If oil drips onto the kitchen floor, throw down salt or baking soda to absorb it before wiping to prevent slips.
300. **Trust your instinct over the recipe clock**: If a recipe says cook for 10 minutes but your eyes tell you it's burning at 5, turn off the heat.
